Guidelines for the Use of Artificial Intelligence

A framework for the responsible, equitable, and effective integration of AI across instruction and operations in Lynn Public Schools — in alignment with LPS Policy IJNDG and DESE guidance.

📄 Download Full Document Explore the Principles

Our Vision: LPS aims to leverage AI-powered tools to empower educators, center student experiences, and improve administrative processes — while continuously evaluating impact and risk to ensure implementation aligns with our core values of equity, access, and opportunity for every learner.

Foundation

Five Core Principles for AI Use

These principles, adapted from DESE guidance and tailored for Lynn, define what responsible AI use looks like in our schools and offices.

🔒

Data Privacy & Security

AI use complies with FERPA, COPPA, and all relevant data governance laws. Only district-approved tools may be used, and protected information is never shared with AI.

What this looks like
  • Only AI tools vetted through a Data Use Agreement are approved.
  • Personal accounts and unapproved platforms are not used for district business.
  • PII, IEPs, health records, and disciplinary data are never entered into AI tools.
  • Annual training on AI data privacy is required for all staff.
👁️

Transparency & Accountability

Educators, students, and families deserve to know when AI is involved in learning, grading, or decisions. Notification happens through general communications like the annual handbook.

What this looks like
  • Schools inform families about classroom AI through handbooks and newsletters — not separate notifications for every use.
  • Educators communicate to students when AI is used for learning or feedback.
  • AI is a productivity aid, never a replacement for educator expertise or required team discussions.
  • Staff explain AI-supported processes in plain language.
⚖️

Bias Awareness & Mitigation

AI tools are critically examined for embedded bias. The district reviews whether certain groups are disproportionately impacted and acts to reduce harm.

What this looks like
  • Procurement includes bias analysis for every new tool.
  • Educators are trained to evaluate AI output for stereotypes and inaccuracies.
  • AI is never the sole basis for placement, discipline, grading, or service decisions.
  • AI access and usage are reviewed for equity — who is using AI, and who isn't.
🧠

Human Oversight & Educator Judgment

AI supports — but does not replace — educators. Educator judgment, empathy, and contextual knowledge remain central to every consequential decision.

What this looks like
  • Educators review and edit all AI-generated materials before use.
  • IEP, 504, and MTSS teams use AI as a support tool — never a decision-maker.
  • Final grading, placement, and instructional decisions are always made by educators.
  • Required team discussions cannot be replaced by AI summaries.
🎓

Academic Integrity

AI is used in ways that reinforce learning, with clear expectations that emphasize originality, transparency, and reflection — not penalties for use.

What this looks like
  • Schools teach students when, how, and why AI tools may be used.
  • Assignments emphasize critical thinking, reflection, and personal voice.
  • Students are taught to cite or acknowledge AI assistance.
  • Focus is on building critical thinking — not catching students who use AI.
Classroom Practice

The LPS AI Use Scale

AI use is a case-by-case decision made by the educator based on the learning goals of each assignment. Educators communicate which level applies at the start of every assignment.

✅

AI Encouraged

Use AI to support your work

Students are encouraged to use generative AI for this assignment.

  • The AI tool is district-approved.
  • The educator has explicitly permitted AI use for the task.
  • Final ideas, understanding, and product must reflect the student's own thinking.
  • Students cite or acknowledge AI use as directed.
⚙️

AI Assisted

Use AI for specific parts

Students may use specific AI tools for designated parts of the work.

  • The AI tool is district-approved.
  • The educator specifies the exact tool that may be used.
  • The educator specifies the part of the assignment where AI is permitted.
  • Final work must be the student's own.
🚫

AI Restricted

No AI use for this work

Students will not use generative AI for this assignment or activity.

  • Any AI use will be treated as academic dishonesty.
  • Educators design assignments that demonstrate authentic student thinking — in-class writing, oral defenses, drafts and revisions.
Guidance by Audience

For Educators & For Students

What responsible AI use looks like in practice — illustrative examples of where AI fits and where it doesn't.

Generative AI Use by Educators, School Leaders & Staff

Generative AI offers numerous opportunities to enhance practice, streamline tasks, personalize learning, and provide additional support for multilingual learners and students with disabilities. Staff must remain vigilant about overreliance, bias, and data privacy — and must maintain human oversight on every consequential decision.

✅ Potential Uses

  • Lesson and unit planning: outlining, generating discussion questions, brainstorming differentiation.
  • Assessment design: drafting quizzes, rubrics, or surveys for educator review.
  • Communication: drafting administrative emails, family newsletters, and announcements.
  • Translation: first-draft translations, always reviewed and verified by a qualified speaker.
  • Adaptations and modifications: suggesting scaffolds and visual supports that maintain grade-level rigor.
  • Documentation: summarizing non-confidential documents and meeting notes.
  • Thought partner: testing ideas and surfacing alternative perspectives.

🚫 Inappropriate Uses

  • Unapproved tools: only district-approved tools may be used for LPS work.
  • Sharing private information: never enter PII, IEPs, health records, or confidential staff data.
  • Replacing professional judgment: AI output must always be reviewed and validated.
  • Decisions without oversight: grading, placement, discipline, and AI-detection accusations require human review.
  • Replacing required team discussions: IEP, 504, and MTSS meetings cannot be replaced by AI summaries.
  • Impersonating others: never use AI to imitate other staff, students, or community members.

Generative AI Use by Students

Students may benefit from generative AI through personalized learning, creativity tools, and language support. Critical thinking is the goal — not catching students who use AI. Our focus is on educating students about how to use AI thoughtfully and how to retain their own voice as learners.

✅ Potential Uses (with educator permission)

  • Brainstorming: exploring different perspectives, expanding on initial ideas.
  • Feedback and revision: using AI as a writing coach to suggest improvements.
  • Accessibility: text-to-speech, alt-text descriptions, language support.
  • Study support: generating practice questions, flashcards, or personalized explanations.
  • Conversation partner: practicing a language, interview, or debate.
  • Executive functioning: coaching on time management and task organization.

🚫 Inappropriate Uses

  • Without permission: using AI when guidelines or the educator restrict it.
  • Replacing critical thinking: using AI in place of skills the assignment is meant to build.
  • On assessments: using AI to complete quizzes, tests, or skill-evaluation assignments.
  • Plagiarism: submitting AI-generated content as one's own without acknowledgment.
  • Harmful content: creating misinformation, deepfakes, or harassing content about others.
⚠️

A note on AI Detection Tools

AI detection tools are currently unreliable and have been shown to bias against multilingual and non-native English writers. LPS strongly discourages using detection tools as the sole indicator of AI use. Any flagged work must be reviewed by a human educator considering past work, writing style, and revision history.

Tools & Approval

Approved AI Tools

All tools are reviewed by the LPS AI Review Committee. Data Use Agreements must be in place before any tool is used with students. The list below is illustrative; the official list is maintained on the LPS AI Hub.

Tool Description Status
Microsoft Copilot General-purpose generative AI assistant integrated with Microsoft 365 (staff use). Approved
Microsoft Copilot for Education Education-specific Copilot features for staff and qualifying students. Pending review
MagicSchool Educator-facing AI tools for lesson planning, differentiation, and feedback. Pending review
Canva (Magic Studio) AI design features within Canva for staff and students. Pending review
Public ChatGPT (free) Public ChatGPT account on chatgpt.com without district data agreement. Not approved

Want to propose a new tool? Submit a request to the LPS AI Review Committee with the proposed use case and audience.
